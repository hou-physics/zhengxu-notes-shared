Welcome

These are working research notes from Zhengxu Hou's MSc thesis, Hydrogen Recombination Lines in Nearby Galaxies: Harvesting the ALMA Archive (Argelander-Institut für Astronomie, Univ. Bonn, supervised by Prof. F. Bertoldi).

The site is built directly from Markdown source on every push to a private repository, so what you see here always reflects the current state of the project.

Reading the page

Three themes

The buttons at the very bottom of the sidebar switch between Light (warm cream, comfortable for long reading), Iceberg (cool slate, better for code-heavy pages), and Dark (low-light work). Your choice is remembered in your browser.

Collapsing the sidebar

The ◀ button at the top of the sidebar collapses it; the icon becomes ☰ so you can re-open it. Useful when reading wide tables or breaking out interactive figures across the full content width.

The right column of every page shows an automatic table of contents that highlights the section currently in view. Clicking entries jumps to that section.

Leaving comments

The site has Hypothes.is embedded — an academic annotation layer. Comments are anchored to the exact words you select, sync to a server in real time, and remain visible to you across sessions on any device.

One-time setup

  1. Look for the small < tab at the top-right edge of any page. Click it to open the Hypothes.is sidebar.
  2. Sign up with email + password (free, ~30 s; the service is run by a non-profit and is permanently free for academic use).
  3. To keep comments restricted to a private group rather than visible on the public web, follow the invitation link Zhengxu provides and join the group.

Each annotation

  1. Select any text in the body of a page.
  2. A small popup appears with an Annotate button — click it.
  3. Type your comment in the sidebar that opens. Important: in the small dropdown at the top of the new annotation, switch the destination from Public to the private group's name before posting, otherwise the comment will appear on the open web.
  4. Click Post. The annotation saves to the server immediately.

When Zhengxu next opens the same page, the highlighted passage and your comment appear in his sidebar. He can reply, mark it resolved, or address the issue in the source and push an update.

Annotation works on any text in the main column — paragraphs, table cells, code lines, equation labels. Mathematical formulas rendered by KaTeX cannot themselves be selected (they're rendered as SVG), but you can always select the surrounding sentence and reference the equation by its number or anchor.

Practical notes

  • Updates: the site rebuilds and republishes automatically whenever the source repository is pushed. There is no fixed release schedule — if a section disappears, it has been moved or merged into a different page.
  • Permanence: URLs to specific pages and to specific section anchors (e.g. …/20-methodology/01-data_selection_method.html#3-r-pred-predictive-ranking-quantity) are stable — you can link back to them from email or other documents.
  • No tracking: the site itself sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and embeds only three third-party resources (the KaTeX maths renderer, the Plotly chart library, and Hypothes.is, all from public CDNs). The Hypothes.is service does of course store annotations server-side, by design.
