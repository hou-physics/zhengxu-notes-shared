Step 6 — The correlation diagram

Step 6 — The correlation diagram

Script: _step3/uniform_batch_stage4_plot.py.

Axes

  • y: recombination-line luminosity, multiplied by n⁶ — the group's convention for placing different lines (H30α, H40α) on one scale. The physical basis: a recombination line's intensity falls as the quantum level n to the −6th power (Gordon & Sorochenko 2002), so multiplying by n⁶ removes the level dependence; H40α points then interleave with H30α points instead of sitting 5.6× low. Our implementation reproduces the group's previous plotted values to 0.05 dex (checked by numerically inverting Lau's published NGC 3628 entry).

  • x: total infrared luminosity, scaled to a matching aperture (previous page).

  • Distances as described in Step 5d — literature values for the nearby sources, Hubble flow for the distant ones.

Markers

  • Colour/shape encodes the line species (double-coded for colour-vision accessibility).
  • Detections: filled symbol ± 1σ bars (calibrated). Marginals: semi-transparent.
  • Upper limits: open symbol drawn at the 3σ value with a downward arrow — the graphical statement "the true value lies anywhere below here". The symbol carries no error bar of its own: the 3σ position already is the uncertainty statement. (Until 2026-08-26 the limits were drawn at the measured flux instead; the change makes the plotted points the same 3σ values the text and the tables quote.)
  • x-errors: IRAS flux-error propagation, plus the 0.2 dex apportioning systematic (Lau's measured value) for every source, since every x-coordinate is apportioned by the 8 μm fraction.

Historical overlay

The plot carries the group's previous results (Lau 2019, Bittner 2022, Bădescu) and the Bittner 2022 linear fit, so every new point is immediately comparable with the lineage. (The historical points are currently digitized from the published figure; replacing them with tabulated values is queued.)

The apportioning decision (settled 2026-08-21)

The thesis apportions the infrared over the tracer aperture itself (the predecessors' method, Step 5c Option B), so both axes refer to the same region. The evidence that settled it: with the field-of-view aperture the five line-bright sources sit one-sidedly 0.80 dex below the historical relation — an aperture-mismatch artefact, not physics — while with the tracer aperture they scatter on both sides (mean −0.12 dex; NGC 4826, reported as an upper limit, is not part of this statistic). The four sources without a usable 8 μm map are excluded from the diagram and remain in the tables; 34 of the 38 measured sources are plotted.

A previously considered third presentation (converting both axes to star-formation rates, as in Bădescu's thesis) was examined and dropped (2026-08-08): it adds a conversion layer without adding information.
