Step 5 — Infrared side and distances

Step 5 — Infrared side and distances

Script: _step3/uniform_batch_stage3_ir.py (+ uniform_batch_iras_errors.py for uncertainties). Everything here is fetched automatically by coordinates — no manual catalog lookups.

4a. Total infrared luminosity (whole galaxy)

Source: the IRAS Revised Bright Galaxy Sample (total-galaxy four-band photometry — the right photometry for galaxies that extend over many IRAS beams); the Point/Faint Source Catalogs serve only as the fallback for the four sources without a usable RBGS entry (Circinus fails its galactic-latitude cut, NGC 5408 lies below its flux limit, He 2-10 is absent, IC 4687 is blended with its interacting pair). Flux densities at 12, 25, 60, 100 μm with published uncertainties. Combined into the total infrared luminosity TIR (8–1000 μm) with the standard Sanders & Mirabel (1996) formula; uncertainties propagated following Lau (2019, Appendix D). Star-formation rates, where quoted, use log SFR = log L_TIR − 43.41 (Murphy et al. 2011).

4b. Infrared size and coverage check

The ALMA field of view is a small circle; the galaxy's infrared emission may be larger. To know whether the infrared and the line measurements can be compared at all, each galaxy gets:

  • a WISE 12 μm and 22 μm cutout (SkyView service, all-sky — this is the guaranteed floor; every target has WISE);
  • a background-subtracted curve of growth (cumulative flux in growing circles) → R50/R90, the radii containing 50%/90% of the light;
  • a coverage verdict measured against an in-sample point-source standard (IRAS 17208−0014, at 183 Mpc necessarily unresolved): if the source's light is as concentrated as the instrument's own point response, it is "covered-unresolved"; otherwise "covered" or "partial" depending on how much falls inside the ALMA field of view.

The 22 μm image sizes carry a caveat: the public WISE Atlas images are smoothed to ~17″ resolution, so compact galaxies hit that floor — flagged "unresolved", never quoted as real sizes.

4c. Scaling the infrared to a matching area: Option A and Option B

The line flux comes from a small nuclear aperture; IRAS measures the whole galaxy. Before the two can share a diagram, the whole-galaxy infrared value must be scaled down to an area that matches the line measurement. Two complete procedures exist in the pipeline. Both start from the same IRAS total; they differ only in which area the total is scaled to and which image provides the spatial information. The choice is settled (2026-08-21): the thesis uses Option B throughout — both axes then refer to the same region. Option A is retained as the coverage cross-check.

Option A — scaling to the ALMA field of view

The area is the telescope's field of view for the observation (the primary-beam circle, 30″ to 60″ for our data). Steps, per galaxy:

  1. Take the WISE 22 μm cutout and its background-subtracted curve of growth (the same products as the coverage check of 4b).
  2. Compare the enclosed-light profile with that of the in-sample point-source standard, so that instrument blur is accounted for.
  3. The scaling factor is the fraction of the galaxy's infrared light enclosed within the primary-beam radius, capped at one. For sources classified "covered" the factor is one by construction.
  4. Aperture-matched value = factor × the IRAS total. Sources with a partial factor carry an additional 0.2 dex systematic uncertainty (the group's measured apportioning scatter).

Character of Option A: it is available for every source (WISE is all-sky), but the area it matches is the field of view, which is larger than the CO aperture where the line is actually measured. The match to the line measurement is therefore approximate.

Option B — scaling to the CO aperture itself (the predecessors' method)

The area is the CO aperture, the exact region of the line measurement. This is the method of Lau (2019, his eq. 2.8) and Bittner (2022, her eq. 5.4), with one repair added by this work. Steps, per galaxy:

  1. Retrieve the Spitzer 8 μm science mosaics for the field, both the deep exposure and the short exposure taken alongside it.
  2. Repair the saturation. The deep mosaics are saturated on bright nuclei, exactly where the CO apertures sit, which silently biases the aperture fraction low. Saturated pixels, plus a 3-pixel rim where the detector response is already nonlinear, are replaced by the short-exposure values; every repair is validated by the deep-to-short flux ratio on bright unsaturated pixels (median ratio 1.00, and within 0.91 to 1.08 wherever the check passes; patches off by more than 10% are flagged, IRAS F05189-2524 the furthest at 0.56). Bittner suspected exactly this problem for NGC 3628 but did not correct it; with the repair her outlier moves back onto the relation.
  3. Reproject the CO aperture from the ALMA pixel grid onto the 8 μm image grid (a world-coordinate transformation).
  4. The scaling factor is the background-subtracted 8 μm flux inside the reprojected aperture divided by the whole-galaxy 8 μm total (from a surface-brightness-truncated curve of growth).
  5. Aperture-matched value = factor × the IRAS total.

Character of Option B: both axes of the diagram then refer to the same region, which is what the underlying physical question (ionizing photons versus star formation in the same place) requires. Its price: it assumes the 8 μm emission traces the total infrared spatially (documented to fail at the extremes, e.g. very extended disks and AGN-dominated nuclei), and it needs an 8 μm map, which four legacy sources lack (NGC 1386, NGC 4569, NGC 7465, NGC 7582); those four are excluded from the correlation diagram — no mixed apertures — and remain in every table.

What the data say about the choice

With Option A the five line-bright sources (the two detections and three marginals) sit on average 0.80 dex below the previous group fit, four of five below: a one-sided offset, traced to the area mismatch between the axes. With Option B they scatter on both sides of the fit (mean −0.12 dex, scatter 0.80 dex, two of five below, individual offsets from +0.8 to −1.4 dex), statistically indistinguishable from the previous results. (Earlier snapshots of this page quoted six-source values −0.03/0.71 that included NGC 4826; the thesis reports that source as an upper limit and keeps it out of this statistic. The pre-repair IRAS-photometry numbers −0.61/+0.07 appeared in still earlier snapshots.)

Why only the 8 μm data can carry the aperture method

The scaling above requires the infrared image to resolve the CO aperture: if the instrument's point-spread function (PSF, the blur disk of the telescope) is larger than the aperture, then "the fraction of light inside the aperture" no longer measures the galaxy's light distribution, it measures the blur itself. The rule of thumb is that the PSF must be comfortably smaller than the aperture diameter.

Our measured CO apertures have equivalent radii of 0.4″ to 13.3″ (median 3.1″), i.e. typical diameters of about 6″ (computed from the aperture areas in the master results table). Against that scale, the available infrared datasets compare as follows:

dataset band(s) angular resolution resolves a ~6″ aperture? role in this survey
IRAS catalog 12 / 25 / 60 / 100 μm ≈ 0.5′ to 2′ (band dependent) no, by a factor 5 to 60 whole-galaxy TIR total only
WISE W3 12 μm 6.5″ (instrument PSF) no, PSF ≈ aperture (a point source already spills half its light outside a 6″ region) backup for the coverage check when W4 fails (used e.g. for NGC 3628)
WISE W4 22 μm 12″ instrument; ≈ 17″ half-light in the Atlas coadds we use (our own measurement) no field-of-view coverage check only, a far easier task: the ALMA field (30–60″) is 2–4× larger than the blur. Chosen over the sharper W3 for physics, not resolution: 22 μm traces the warm star-formation-heated dust that follows the total infrared
Spitzer MIPS 24 μm ≈ 6″ marginal, PSF ≈ aperture fallback candidate only, not used
Spitzer IRAC (SEIP) 8 μm ≈ 2″ yes, ~3× smaller than the median aperture the apportioning template

Herschel (PACS, ≈ 6″ to 11″ at 70–160 μm) sits in the same marginal class as MIPS and its archival coverage is incomplete for our sample; it is not used.

So the chain of instruments is matched to the chain of scales: IRAS measures the whole galaxy (it cannot do anything else), WISE is just sharp enough to resolve the 30–60″ ALMA field of view for the coverage check, and only the 2″ Spitzer 8 μm imaging can resolve the CO aperture itself, which is why the contour-aperture scaling exists in the 8 μm band and in no other. This is also why 8 μm coverage is a hard requirement in the sample selection (Step 1).

One honest boundary of the method: for the most distant, most compact sources the aperture radius approaches 2″, where even the 8 μm PSF is at its limit. There the source and the aperture are both essentially point-like, the enclosed fraction saturates toward one, and the scaling degrades gracefully rather than silently.

4d. Distances

Luminosity = flux × 4πD², so every luminosity needs a distance.

  • Distant sources (recession velocity ≥ 3500 km/s, 14 galaxies): Hubble-flow distance D = cz/H₀ (H₀ = 70). At these velocities the galaxy's own peculiar motion (a few hundred km/s) is a < 10% effect.
  • Nearby sources (25 galaxies): the Hubble-flow estimate can be wrong by a factor of ~2 (Centaurus A: 7.8 vs. true 3.8 Mpc) or undefined (NGC 4569 is blueshifted). For these, every published redshift-independent distance was harvested from NED-D (361 measurements) and one value adopted per galaxy by a fixed rule: the median of the best-ranked method, ranked TRGB > Cepheids > SBF > SNIa > Tully–Fisher (TRGB = tip of the red giant branch, the most reliable of these). Adopted values with method, number of measurements and reference: result_v2/_uniform_batch/adopted_distances.csv; full audit trail: distance_candidates.csv. Scripts: _step3/distance_table_fetch.py + distance_adopt.py.

Two consequences worth knowing: the largest distance revision in the sample is NGC 4945 (8.05 → 3.65 Mpc, TRGB), and NGC 3628 moves from its flow value of 11.9 Mpc to the adopted 9.7 Mpc (Tully–Fisher) — though its landing on the historical relation is chiefly the 8 μm saturation repair above, not the distance. NGC 4569, blueshifted and therefore without a flow distance, carries its literature distance (17.0 Mpc) in the tables; it stays off the correlation diagram for lack of an 8 μm map.
