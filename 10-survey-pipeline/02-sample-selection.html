Step 1 — Sample selection

Step 1 — Sample selection

The sample is built by intersecting three public archives, in this order. No step anywhere asks "is the recombination line likely to be detectable?" — that would bias the survey toward detections.

1. Parent catalog: IRAS bright galaxies (60 μm)

Source: the IRAS Revised Bright Galaxy Sample (RBGS; Sanders et al. 2003), VizieR table J/AJ/126/1607 — 629 galaxies, complete above a 60 μm flux of f60 > 5.24 Jy (ten times the IRAS survey's faint limit).

Why this catalog: the correlation's x-axis is infrared luminosity, so a flux-limited infrared parent gives the survey a well-defined selection function on exactly that axis, while remaining blind to the y-axis (the recombination line).

2. Sky-position filter: DEC ≤ +40°

An engineering filter, not a scientific one. ALMA sits at latitude −23°; a source at declination +40° never rises above 27° elevation, and in practice the archive contains almost nothing above that line.

3. Infrared-resolution requirement: Spitzer 8 μm coverage

Source: Spitzer Enhanced Imaging Products (SEIP), queried automatically against the IRSA slphotdr4 source list. Script: target_list_build/rbgs_seip_intersect.py.

Why 8 μm: the infrared luminosity must eventually be scaled to the small central region where the line is measured (page 6). Of all infrared surveys, only Spitzer's 8 μm band (~2″ resolution) can resolve those regions; IRAS (~arcminutes) and WISE 22 μm (~12″) cannot. Galaxies without an 8 μm map cannot receive an aperture-matched infrared value.

629 → 501 (after DEC) → 305 with 8 μm coverage.

4. ALMA archive search

Script: target_list_build/alma_scout.py (queries the ALMA Science Archive via astroquery.alma).

For each of the 305 galaxies the script asks: does public, single-pointing ALMA data exist whose frequency coverage contains a recombination line plus a bright tracer line (CO, CS or HCO⁺) at the galaxy's redshift? It records the best angular resolution, the archive-reported sensitivity (noise per 10 km/s channel — the same quantity we later measure ourselves, allowing a promised-vs-delivered comparison), and the download size.

305 → 131 galaxies with usable archival data.

Mosaicked observations (multiple stitched pointings) are excluded here via the archive's is_mosaic flag — the measurement procedure is defined for single pointings only. This is the only hard cut before download; softer indicators (e.g. how centrally concentrated the WISE light is) are recorded as advisory flags but never exclude a galaxy by themselves. The philosophy, fixed after one galaxy (NGC 5236) crossed a would-be threshold: predictions rank, only rules kill — a wrongly-admitted galaxy costs one download and is then excluded by an objective rule; a wrongly-excluded galaxy is invisible forever.

5. Download

Per target we download only the pipeline's actual diet — the calibrated image cubes (*.cube.I.pbcor.fits) and primary-beam maps (*.pb.fits.gz) — not the full archive tarballs; this is 3–50× smaller. The first batch of 16 new targets ("wave 1", ~345 GB) was processed in August 2026; the remaining 95 candidates are queued in the survey registry with per-target size estimates.

One lesson from wave 1, now a documented rule: two targets (NGC 7552, NGC 6814) turned out to have the line outside the observed band at their true redshift — the archive search had matched line frequencies at rest wavelength. The re-scout for the next wave matches redshifted frequencies and treats catalog redshifts of faint distant galaxies as uncertain by a few hundred km/s (see the next page for why).
