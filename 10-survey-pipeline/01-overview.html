Overview: what the survey does

Overview: what the survey does

Goal. A uniform, unbiased survey of millimetre hydrogen recombination lines (HRLs — here H30α at 231.90 GHz and H40α at 99.02 GHz) in nearby galaxies, using only data that already exists in the ALMA archive. Every galaxy is processed by the same frozen procedure — no per-galaxy tuning anywhere — and ends up as either a flux ± uncertainty or a meaningful upper limit on the L(HRL)–L(TIR) correlation diagram. The systematisation of this procedure is itself the central contribution of the thesis.

Why "uniform" matters. If you tune parameters per galaxy until a signal appears, you cannot tell afterwards whether the signal was real or manufactured by the tuning. Under the frozen procedure the only per-galaxy branches are rule-driven exits ("no tracer detected → human review"), each of which is logged. A telltale sign that this works: the signal-to-noise values of the 32 signal-free sources scatter near-symmetrically around zero (11 negative, 21 positive), exactly as honest noise should. The width of that same distribution is itself a measurement: it shows the formal uncertainties to be too small by a factor 1.43, so every σ and S/N the survey quotes carries a global calibration factor of 1.5 (rounded up, the conservative direction), and the detection tiers are cut on the calibrated scale.

The pipeline in one paragraph

A galaxy enters from the sample selection (page 2). Its ALMA data is queried, selectively downloaded, organized under canonical names, and its redshift verified against our own data (page 3) — then processed: memory-mapped cube handling, primary-beam bookkeeping, moment maps and single-pass aperture spectra, with a full re-imaging fallback from raw visibilities (page 4). From the processed cubes a measurement aperture is built on a bright molecular line — CO or CS — and the recombination-line flux is integrated inside it (page 5). The infrared side attaches a matched total-infrared luminosity and a literature distance (page 6). Both quantities meet on the correlation diagram (page 7). Results accumulate in one machine-generated survey registry covering every target the survey has touched or could touch (see the Survey registry section).

Current status (2026-08-26)

  • 39 galaxies fully processed; 38 measured (2 detections, 3 marginal, 33 upper limits on the calibrated scale), 1 under review (Centaurus A).
  • 34 of the 38 appear on the correlation diagram; the four without a usable Spitzer 8 μm map are excluded from the diagram (no mixed apertures) and remain in all tables.
  • 95 further candidates with usable archival data are queued in the registry.
  • The infrared-axis convention is settled (the predecessors' aperture, see page 6); the full thesis draft is written, compiled, and has passed a cross-chapter final review (2026-08-26).

A note on notation used across these pages: primary beam = the telescope's field of view for a single pointing (a circle, ~30–60″ for our data); moment-0 map = an image made by summing the data cube over a velocity range; W20 = the full width of a spectral line measured at 20% of its peak height; TIR = total infrared luminosity (8–1000 μm); dex = a factor of 10 to the given power (0.2 dex ≈ a factor 1.6).
