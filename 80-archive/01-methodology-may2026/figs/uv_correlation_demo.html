1. Antenna array

N antennas randomly placed on a circular patch (mimicking an ALMA configuration).

2. u–v coverage

All baselines (antenna pair separations). Each red dot is one sampled point in the Fourier plane — everything else is unsampled.

3a. Synthesised beam (= inverse FFT of u–v)

The PSF of this telescope: a point source in the sky looks like this in the image — central peak surrounded by sidelobe rings. Contrast clipped (top 3 % saturated) so the rings are visible; without clipping the central peak dominates and the rings compress to near-white.

3b. Pure-noise dirty image (ALMA)

Independent complex Gaussian noise injected only at the sampled u–v points, inverse-FFT'd to the image plane. The noise comes out with visible blobs at the beam scale.

4. Pure white noise reference

Same 64×64 Gaussian noise field, but generated directly in the image plane, no u–v sampling, no FFT — every pixel an independent measurement. This is what 3b would look like if ALMA sampled every u–v point. The contrast between 3b (blobs at beam scale) and 4 (no structure) is exactly what "interferometric pixels are correlated" means.

5. Noise + discrete residual outliers

Same ALMA noise as 3b, with a dozen isolated outlier pixels sprinkled at random positions (each 4–7σ noise amplitude). Pick a contamination scenario below — each maps to a real failure mode that produces non-Gaussian pixels in real ALMA data.

6. Histograms (log y axis exposes tails) — well-behaved vs contaminated

Two histograms side by side, both with log y axis. Look at |value| > 3σ. Left (well-behaved): 3b and 4 sit on top of the Gaussian reference all the way out — correlated noise has the same per-pixel distribution as independent noise. Right (contaminated): panel 5 (off-source pixels only, standardised by the pure-noise σ of 3b) clearly overshoots the Gaussian beyond ~3σ — those extra pixels are deterministic sidelobe ripples masquerading as noise.

More antennas → more baselines → denser u–v sampling → sharper beam → smaller noise blobs (less per-pixel correlation). Few antennas → sparse u–v → fat beam → big correlated noise blobs. The relationship between beam size and noise correlation length is the central reason interferometric pixels aren't independent.
