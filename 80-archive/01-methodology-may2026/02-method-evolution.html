Method evolution (April–May 2026)

Snapshot 2026-05-22, prepared for Prof. F. Bertoldi.

This page traces how the H$n\alpha$ detection method evolved from mid-April to mid-May 2026 — six weeks during which the pipeline went from a hand-tuned single-galaxy script to a per-source-class framework covering 32 archival galaxies. Each step below marks a concrete failure mode the data forced us to confront and the methodological response. Numbers and galaxy lists are summarised only where they illustrate the change; the underlying issue log is in the private notebook.

1. Calibration on a single galaxy (early April)

The pipeline started as a hand-tuned analysis of NGC 3628 — H30$\alpha$ in a single ALMA cube, with a manually-drawn signal box and a velocity window coupled to the CO FWHM (FWHM × vthresh).

Two findings from this period changed the methodology rather than just the code:

  1. Briggs vs. natural weighting changes the apparent CO line width (NGC 3628 CO FWHM $160 \to 240$ km/s when re-imaged from the same visibilities). Any velocity-window rule tied to the CO FWHM was therefore implicitly tied to the imaging choice. The velocity window was replaced by an absolute width scan over $60$–$500$ km/s in $20$ km/s steps — $23$ widths, independent of the strong-tracer imaging.
  2. Baseline subtraction at the $\sim 0.5$ Jy·km/s level matters on weak sources. The legacy "no baseline subtraction" path overestimated NGC 3628 by exactly that amount. Per-channel baseline subtraction (median over $\sigma$-clipped noise channels, matching the Ada (2022) / Bittner (2022) recipe) was adopted as the default before any line integration.

Take-away: the scan parameters and the imaging products must be decoupled. Anything that secretly depended on a weighting choice was a methodology landmine.

2. From one galaxy to the archive (21–24 April)

ALMA archive products do not look like our custom-imaged NGC 3628 cube. The HRL and the strong-tracer line typically sit in separate per-SPW FITS files, often at different angular resolutions and pixel scales. The pipeline was extended to a dual-cube mode: the strong-tracer cube is convolved to the common beam, the resulting mask is reprojected onto the HRL grid via reproject_interp, and the flux is then integrated on the HRL cube with the transferred mask. The single-cube case degenerates to identity operations.

Two methodology rules were tightened in this batch:

  • Self-masking is forbidden. A first NGC 6810 run had silently used the HRL cube itself as the mask source (because the CO line was in a different SPW that wasn't being loaded). The result was a spurious $\mathrm{S/N} = 2.36$ "detection". The rule is now explicit: the spatial mask must come from an independent strong tracer (CO(2-1) / CO(1-0) / HCN / HCO+ / CS) in the same observation, and the pipeline raises a hard error if the HRL and strong-tracer FITS headers fail a coverage check.
  • The flux convention is fixed at the source. Mask is built from the strong-tracer non-pbcor cube (uniform noise across the primary-beam footprint); the HRL line flux is integrated on the pbcor cube. Mixing the two has $20$–$80\%$ flux consequences on any non-trivial primary-beam taper.

3. The selector-bias problem (24–25 April)

The headline detection statistic is the maximum signal-to-noise ratio over an $\sim 828$-combination scan: $3$ spatial-smoothing kernels $\times$ $12$ spatial thresholds ($3$–$32\,\sigma$ in logspace plus a dense $14$–$19\,\sigma$ over-sampling) $\times$ $23$ velocity widths ($60$–$500$ km/s in $20$ km/s steps). Two well-documented failure modes showed that this "max-S/N over a scan grid" statistic is statistically meaningless on cubes that do not contain a real source, and one exposed the same problem on a real source:

Source Mode Why max-S/N misled
NGC 7793 Off-pointing, diffuse CO Mask grew to $1250$ beams at the lowest $\sigma$ threshold, integrating primary-beam edge noise — the optimiser preferred a huge sub-threshold envelope to a small clean one
NGC 5253 native $0.19''$ Source IS real CO footprint $\sigma_\text{unsmoothed}$ was $\sim 0.3\times$ source peak — the $3\sigma$ footprint fragmented into $142$ components, and the connected-component filter kept only a $4$-beam fragment, missing the real source

The response was three independent diagnostics that any non-detection must now also pass — they do not replace the scan, but they expose its failures:

  1. CO footprint hard constraint. Every candidate mask in the scan is AND-ed with the physically confirmed CO footprint $\{\text{CO mom-0} > 3\sigma\}$, smoothed to a fixed $1''$ scale first so the noise reference is per resolution element rather than per pixel. The smoothing fix recovered NGC 5253 native from $\mathrm{S/N}\,{=}\,{-}0.26$ to $\mathrm{S/N}\,{=}\,9.4$ at $0.19''$ beam. (The AND-step was retired on 23 May 2026 — see §9. The smoothed-CO $> 3\sigma$ region is still where the scan-best mask gets defined; the change is that it is no longer additionally clipped against a fixed reference scale.)
  2. Fixed-envelope diagnostic. A parallel measurement at the default mask ($3 \times \text{bmaj}$ smoothing, $3\sigma$ threshold, $W = 333$ km/s). A genuine detection produces consistent sign and amplitude between the scan-optimised and fixed-envelope numbers; a selector-bias non-detection produces a sign flip — the classical fingerprint of a $\max$-over-grid noise pickup.
  3. IoU(mask, CO footprint) as morphology label. IoU (intersection-over-union) is the standard image-segmentation overlap metric, $\text{IoU}(A, B) = |A \cap B| / |A \cup B| \in [0, 1]$. Because the scan mask is hard-constrained to lie inside the CO footprint ($A \subseteq B$), the intersection is just $A$ and the union is just $B$, so IoU here reduces to the fill fraction of the footprint: how much of the physical CO footprint does the detected mask actually occupy? Four quadrants in (S/N, IoU) sort the sample cleanly: high IoU + high S/N = extended detection (NGC 253, IoU $\sim 0.57$), low IoU + high S/N = compact-nuclear detection (NGC 3628, IoU $\sim 0.03$), high IoU + low S/N = extended weak non-detection (M83, IoU $\sim 0.78$), low IoU + low S/N = selector-bias non-detection (NGC 7582, IoU $\sim 0.09$). (IoU was retired as a catalogue column on 23 May 2026 — see §9. In practice it changed the verdict on only four marginal galaxies that the per-galaxy null + fixed-envelope diagnostic also caught.)

4. Two complementary null tests (22 April → 13 May)

Two independent null statistics are now run on every candidate detection.

The sample-wide null ($P_{95}^{\text{all}}$) calibrates the "effective $3\sigma$" of the scan after the look-elsewhere penalty. Each galaxy contributes the scan-best $\mathrm{S/N}$ measured at $\sim 16$ line-free velocity offsets (with the strong-tracer mask and noise box held fixed); the $95^\text{th}$ percentile of those values over the threshold-group sample is the effective $3\sigma$. Post-fix this sits at $P_{95}^{\text{all}} \approx 1.5$ (see §6 for the factor- of-two correction).

The per-galaxy null ($P_{95}^{\text{gal}}$) does the same procedure within a single cube. It is the only diagnostic that detects galaxy-specific contamination — bandpass residuals at a particular SPW, continuum knots persisting across velocity offsets, or short-spacing artefacts. NGC 5135 (H30$\alpha$) is the worked example: its scan-best $5.64\,\sigma$ passed every other diagnostic, but the per-galaxy null revealed two contamination families (a velocity-independent compact continuum knot at offsets $-300$ to $-406$ km/s, and a smoothly drifting baseline residual at $+300$ to $+522$ km/s), each generating $5$–$6\,\sigma$ off-line peaks. The classification standard adopted is a dual-tier criterion on the per-galaxy null: a source is a clean detection when $\mathrm{S/N} \geq 2 \times P_{95}^{\text{gal}}$ and visually identifiable in the moment-$0$ map, tentative when $\mathrm{S/N} \geq P_{95}^{\text{gal}}$ but below the $2\times$ margin, and non-detection otherwise. The factor of two is the empirical margin above which the per-galaxy null + fixed-envelope diagnostic have never disagreed on any source we have inspected by eye.

Why we quote P95, not a Gaussian 3σ

The "effective $3\sigma$" terminology is loose — strict Gaussian $3\sigma$ corresponds to a false-positive rate of $0.27\%$, but our scan-best $\mathrm{S/N}$ does not follow Gaussian statistics, for two independent reasons:

  1. Pixel-level Gaussianity is broken by the imaging. ALMA does not measure pixels directly; it measures visibilities in the $(u, v)$ plane, and the image is the inverse Fourier transform of those visibilities. Pixels within one synthesised beam are not independent samples — they are the same Fourier-noise sample spread across pixels by the beam. Treating each pixel as an independent measurement under-estimates the noise by $\sqrt{\text{beam area in pixels}}$, typically a factor of $\sim 6$.
  2. The pixel histogram is contaminated by systematics that have non-Gaussian tails. Companion lines in the same SPW (the Circinus SO + H$50\beta$ event), incomplete continuum subtraction, CLEAN over-subtraction (negative bowls around bright extended sources), bandpass artefacts — these contributions are deterministic and sparse. They produce true non-Gaussian tails (excess kurtosis $>0$) and, when one-sided, asymmetric tails (non-zero skewness).

The per-galaxy null test handles both problems empirically: by re-running the scan at line-free velocity offsets, it samples the actual noise distribution under the actual scan procedure on this specific cube. The $95^\text{th}$ percentile of those measurements is therefore the "empirical equivalent of $3\sigma$" — not a textbook Gaussian threshold, but exactly the threshold our data warrants.

The widget below makes both points tangible. The top half (antenna array → $u$–$v$ coverage → synthesised beam → dirty image) shows why pixels are correlated: sparser $u$–$v$ coverage produces a wider beam, which produces larger noise blobs and stronger correlation between neighbouring pixels. The bottom half (white-noise reference, contamination scenarios, pixel-value histograms) shows that correlation is not the same as non-Gaussianity — correlated noise still has a Gaussian marginal distribution, while sparse outliers from real systematics produce fat tails that no correlation-aware threshold would catch by itself.

5. Sample-selection priors and the multi-line search (1 May)

After 27 galaxies had been processed (9 detections, 17 non-detections), the empirical regime was clear enough to bake into the archive query:

  • Inclination $i \geq 47^\circ$ — no detection has ever been observed below this in the sample, consistent with line-of-sight emission-measure suppression for face-on disks.
  • Beam size $\theta_\text{beam} \cdot D \in [15, 150]$ pc — outside this range we are either resolving out the diffuse component (large $\theta_\text{beam}\,D$, e.g. NGC 5248 at $25\,\text{pc}$ per beam in a $10\,\text{kpc}$ disk) or filtering out the extended emission altogether (small $\theta_\text{beam}\,D$, e.g. NGC 2903 at $5\,\text{pc}$ per beam through long-baseline-only configs).
  • Distance $D \leq 50$ Mpc — the $1/D^2$ flux dilution overwhelms archival sensitivity beyond this even for true LIRGs.

A complementary change opened a new line pair: the original query was restricted to H30$\alpha$ + CO(2-1) in Band 6, but the same observation geometry exists in Band 3 with H40$\alpha$ + CS(2-1) (line separation $1.04$ GHz inside a single $1.875$ GHz SPW). Both new detections from the second half of the campaign — Hen 2-10 and Circinus — entered through this Band 3 channel. The original Band-6-only query would have missed them entirely.

6. Selector bias inside the null itself — Circinus (13 May)

Circinus' first per-galaxy null gave $P_{95}^{\text{gal}} = 10.20$, making the real-line $\mathrm{S/N}\,{=}\,10.43$ look marginal. The investigation surfaced two compounding flaws in the null procedure.

Flaw 1 — re-scanning at every offset injects selection bias. The original null re-ran the full $828$-combination scan at every velocity offset and reported its $\max\,\mathrm{S/N}$. Mathematically this is "the largest of $\sim 10^3$ correlated noise samples", with an expected upward bias of $\sqrt{2 \ln N} \sim 4\sigma$ on pure noise. Fix: lock the scan-best $(s_*, t_*, W_*)$ from the real-line offset and sample only the velocity-offset axis. Circinus $P_{95}^{\text{gal}}$ dropped $10.20 \to 4.88$.

Flaw 2 — same-SPW companion lines were being counted as null noise. Circinus' SPW3 also contains H50$\beta$ at $\Delta v = +612$ km/s and SO ($N_J = 2_3 \to 1_2$) at $+839$ km/s; the latter is brighter than the H40$\alpha$ line. The original $\pm 300$ km/s science-zone exclusion only protected the primary HRL. Fix: a curated catalogue of $\sim 50$ rest frequencies is now subtracted from the available offset axis on every cube; offsets that fall on any known line zone are excluded. Circinus $P_{95}^{\text{gal}}$ dropped further to $1.12$ — Gaussian-noise- consistent.

Two bonus serendipitous detections from the corrected procedure on the same Circinus cube: SO ($2_3\,\text{–}\,1_2$) at $\mathrm{S/N}\,{\approx}\,16$ (spatially-resolved cross-validation of the single-dish measurement by Costagliola+ 2011) and H50$\beta$ at $\mathrm{S/N}\,{=}\,3.0$, with a flux ratio $F(\mathrm{H}50\beta) / F(\mathrm{H}40\alpha) = 0.18$ within a factor $1.5$ of the Storey & Hummer 1995 LTE prediction.

The same correction propagated to the sample-wide null: the original $P_{95}^{\text{all}} = 3.08$ was inflated by $\sim 2\times$ from Flaw 1 alone. The corrected value is $\approx 1.5$.

7. A pre-registration attempt — and why it was rolled back (14 May)

After the Circinus correction, a single-hypothesis recipe was locked in: a fixed mask geometry (smoothing $1 \times \text{bmaj}$, threshold $3\sigma$, connected-component policy per host class), a fixed integration window ($1 \times$ FWHM of the mask-integrated line), and a single analytic $\mathrm{S/N} = F / \sigma_\text{int}$. The intent was to remove the residual scan-induced look-elsewhere penalty entirely and quote a clean, scan-free significance.

Within a few hours of running the locked recipe against the validation set, four known-real detections broke:

Galaxy Old $\mathrm{S/N}$ Locked-recipe $\mathrm{S/N}$ Failure mode
Hen 2-10 $7.89$ mask construction failed Default $3\sigma$ threshold returned no connected component on the compact CS(2-1) footprint
NGC 5253 X1a $9.4$, flux $0.787$ Jy·km/s $-$, flux $0.052$ Jy·km/s Strong-tracer mask spanned only $2.6$ beams; FWHM collapsed to $18$ km/s, $15\times$ flux loss
NGC 3628 $15.81$ $4.46$ Locked mask captured the inner $\sim 1$ beam only, missing the disk-rotation flux
NGC 3627 $4.42$ $0.96$ Locked $1\times$-FWHM window missed the actual mask-integrated line width

The rollback the same day was explicit: a single recipe cannot simultaneously handle compact-nuclear sources (NGC 5253 X1a: sub-arcsec, $< 100$ pc emission region), dwarf compact starbursts (Hen 2-10: a default $3\sigma$ threshold on its CS(2-1) footprint does not even return a connected component), and well-resolved extended disks (NGC 3628: ordered rotation across the integration window, so a fixed-width window inevitably misses flux). The scan framework was restored, and the recipe code is retained only as a diagnostic comparison.

The lesson — recorded so it does not have to be re-learned — is that pre-registration is only legitimate after the recipe survives an independent validation set. Locking a recipe before that point trades a real selection-bias problem for a worse recipe-induced power-loss problem.

8. Per-class tooling and the canonical flux (17 May → present)

The current framework explicitly drops the "one universal pipeline" goal in favour of a divide-and-conquer per source class. Each class — compact-nuclear, extended-disk, multi-component, non-detection — gets its own appropriate diagnostic chain. Detection (yes/no) is still decided by the scan-based $\mathrm{S/N}$ against the per-galaxy null, but the flux quoted in the catalogue now uses a uniform truth-flux estimator:

$$ \mathcal{F}_\text{clean} = \mathcal{F}_\text{signal} - \big\langle \mathcal{F} \text{ at } K = 16 \text{ random spatial-null positions} \big\rangle, $$

where the signal aperture is an intensity-weighted-PCA-fitted ellipse on the $3\sigma$ connected seed (so the aperture has no per-galaxy hand-tuning) and the null positions are random spatial offsets (not velocity offsets — the point is to characterise the local background under the same aperture). Validation: NGC 4945 matches Toma (2024) to $0.1$–$2\%$ in flux; NGC 3628 matches Toma to $2$–$6\%$.

A parallel diagnostic — per-pixel baseline-residual subtraction along the mask centre-of-gravity path — was deployed in the same week. It adds a new systematic-uncertainty column to the catalogue (typical $|F_\text{res}|/\sigma \leq 1.1$ over the validation set) without moving any detection thresholds. The CoG fingerprints flagged by this diagnostic come from mask morphology or CLEAN sidelobes, not from baseline residuals — i.e. the baseline subtraction itself has been adequate all along; the diagnostic just makes that quantitative.

9. Methodology subtraction (23 May)

After the per-class divide-and-conquer pivot in §8 the project paused to audit which steps were doing measurable work versus surviving as belt-and-suspenders, and adopted subtraction by default: any step that has to be defended on conceptual grounds rather than by naming the galaxies whose verdict it changes is a candidate for removal.

Two steps were retired on this round:

  • The CO footprint AND-constraint (§3, item 1). It was originally introduced in late April to fix the NGC 7793 huge-mask and NGC 1313 one-beam-hotspot incidents. The week it shipped, an independent rule was also tightened: the spatial mask must come from an independent strong tracer (CO), not from the HRL cube itself. That rule already eliminates the most violent failure mode — self-masking — that the footprint constraint would otherwise prevent. With self-masking forbidden by construction and the scan picking the smoothing × σ-threshold combination that maximises S/N, the scan-best mask sits inside the would-be footprint anyway. The AND-step changed the verdict on zero galaxies in the 32-source sample. Removed.
  • The IoU diagnostic column (§3, item 3). It changed the verdict on four marginal-band galaxies (NGC 7582, NGC 5643, NGC 1377, IRAS F18293-3413), all of which the per-galaxy null and the fixed-envelope diagnostic also moved from "tentative" to "selector-bias non-detection". The four-quadrant interpretation was pedagogically nice but the column itself wasn't load-bearing. Removed.

What remains as the load-bearing diagnostic chain: mask-from-CO rule (the structural safeguard against self-masking), per-galaxy null test (§4, the detection threshold), fixed-envelope diagnostic (§3, item 2, the parallel mask-shape sanity check), baseline-residual diagnostic (§8, the systematic-uncertainty column), and canonical ellipse-CoG flux (§8, the catalogue flux). The full retrospective on the two retired steps is on the footprint-and-IoU page.

The broader implication, adopted as a project-wide stance: when the next methodology question arises, the default is to subtract a step, not to add one. New steps need to name the galaxies whose verdict they would change vs. the existing pipeline — "conceptually it helps" is not a sufficient justification on its own.

Where it stands today (23 May)

  • 8 clean detection rows across 7 distinct galaxies: NGC 253, NGC 4945, NGC 3628, NGC 3627, NGC 5253 (independently confirmed in two different cubes: cross-mousid $0.91''$ and native $0.19''$), Hen 2-10, Circinus. The strong-calibration detections agree with published values within their respective absolute-flux calibration budgets: NGC 4945 vs Toma (2024) at $4.7\%$, NGC 3628 vs Toma at $\sim 2\%$, NGC 5253 X1a vs Bendo (2017) at $6\%$.
  • 2 marginal cases: IRAS F18293-3413 and NGC 7582 (selector-bias non-detection under the joint diagnostic, scan-best $\mathrm{S/N}$ inside the marginal band).
  • 1 deferred case: NGC 5135. Per-galaxy null exposed two SPW contamination families; classification deferred pending re-imaging from the visibilities.

An interactive side-by-side comparison of representative detections under different methodologies (pre-footprint / pre-null / pre-line-aware-null), with the same cube and the same coloured-mom-0 panels, so that the "what changed and why it changed" of each step above is visible on the figure rather than just narrated in text.
  2. A worked NGC 5253 trifecta (cross-mousid $0.91''$, native $0.19''$, Bendo-class published) demonstrating that the matched- filter S/N hierarchy predicted by the cube geometry actually reproduces in the data — the strongest single calibration of the pipeline.
