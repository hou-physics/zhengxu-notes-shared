Snapshot 2026-05-19, prepared for Prof. F. Bertoldi

2. ALMA archive query (/alma-query)

For each galaxy the workflow is:

  1. NED coordinate resolution + ALMA archive 60″ cone-search.
  2. Line-coverage prerequisite: keep only observations covering both an HRL (H$n\alpha$, $n \in \{27\text{–}32,\;39\text{–}42\}$) and a same-band strong-tracer line (CO(2-1) / CO(1-0) / HCN(3-2) / HCO+(3-2)). The strong-tracer line is used only to define the spatial mask.
  3. Multi-line search added 2026-05-01: besides the default H30α + CO(2-1) (Band 6), we also search for H26α + HCN(4-3) (B7), H33α + HCN(2-1) (B5), and H40α + CS(2-1) (B3) joint same-SPW pairs. Both the Hen 2-10 and Circinus detections enter through the H40α + CS(2-1) channel.
  4. Sensitivity threshold: reject observations with archive-reported $\text{rms}_{10\,\text{km/s}} > 30$ mJy. For a typical HRL flux $0.1$–$5$ Jy·km/s, line FWHM $\sim 200$ km/s, and a $5$–$10$ beam mask, this yields $\sigma_F \approx 3$–$4$ Jy·km/s (per-beam $1.3 \times \sqrt{N_\text{beam}} \approx 2.2$–$3.2$ factor), giving a $3\sigma$ floor of $\sim 10$ Jy·km/s — the strongest typical source would not exceed $3\sigma$.
  5. Geometric hard filter (introduced 2026-05-01; DEC criterion added 2026-05-13 after the M82 incident; see §5).
  6. Sensitivity ranking: sort by sensitivity_10kms; within a 1.5× tie, prefer single-pointing over mosaic.
  7. Output: ranked markdown report + step-1_download.py download script.

3. R_pred — predictive ranking quantity

After line-coverage filtering, the PHANGS + ALMaQUEST + Lau pool yields several hundred (galaxy × observation × H$n\alpha$) triplets. We need a single number to rank them from "most likely to detect" to "least likely":

$$ R_\text{pred} \;=\; \log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\text{SFR}}{M_\odot\,\text{yr}^{-1}}\right) \;-\; 2\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{D}{\text{Mpc}}\right) \;-\; \log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\text{rms}_{10\,\text{km/s}}}{\text{mJy}}\right) $$

Physical basis for each term:

  • SFR coefficient $+1$: under Case B in HII regions, $L_\text{HRL} \propto$ ionising-photon rate $\propto$ SFR (Murphy+ 2011 line-SFR calibration; Storey & Hummer 1995 emission coefficients). Absolute coefficients ($T_e$, filling factor, beam–source geometry) cancel for relative ranking within a homogeneous nearby sample.
  • D coefficient $-2$: for an unresolved source, $F \propto 1/D^2$. This is the root of $R_\text{pred}$'s breakdown for resolved disks — galaxy-integrated SFR does not translate to per-beam HRL surface brightness.
  • rms coefficient $-1$: $\text{S/N} \propto 1/\text{rms}$.

The $-2 \log D$ term implicitly assumes the source fits inside one beam. The figure below makes the failure of that assumption visible — drag the sliders and watch the saturated disk (what one beam collects) stop growing once the source overflows the beam.

R_pred limitations

Issue Consequence
Uses galaxy-integrated SFR, not per-beam HRL surface brightness Smooth-disk galaxies fall below the per-beam 3σ floor
Inclination ignored (HRL path length $\propto \sec i$) Face-on galaxies are not seen through
Beam–source geometry matching not considered Long-baseline configurations resolve out diffuse sources
rms term uses the archive map-centre value Sources near the primary-beam edge experience reduced effective σ

$R_\text{pred}$ works for relative ranking within a homogeneous morphological sub-sample, but breaks across the "compact nuclear ↔ smooth disk" boundary. The asymmetry of its failure is worth spelling out.

At large $D$, the source is unresolved by virtue of distance, but the integrated luminosity is suppressed by $1/D^2$. The ALMaQUEST sample at $D = 40\text{–}260$ Mpc bottoms out this regime — 0 of 46 sources reached the download stage.

At small $D$ with extended structure, the source is resolved across many beams and per-beam flux saturates at the surface-brightness ceiling $I_\nu \cdot \Omega_\text{beam}$, independent of $D$ (figure above). The $-2 \log D$ term keeps rising past this point, so the ranker over-counts these sources. NGC 5248 is the canonical case: $R_\text{pred}$ ranked it 2 of 8 in the PHANGS pool, but its $\sim 30''$ disk is fully resolved out by the 12 m C43-9/10 long-baseline configuration ($\text{MRS} \sim 2\text{–}3''$).

The favourable regime — compact + nearby. "Unresolved" can be reached by two paths: small $D$ with a physically small source, or large $D$ with any source. Only the first path helps detection — it concentrates the full luminosity into a single beam at small $D$, maximising per-beam flux. The second is just $1/D^2$ dilution. The IRAS pivot (§4) selects on warm $S_{25}/S_{60}$ colour precisely because that cut singles out compact nuclear starbursts — giving the geometric small-$D$ benefit without the smooth-disk saturation penalty. NGC 5253 X1a ($r \sim 30$ pc, $D = 3.8$ Mpc), Circinus ($r \sim 100$ pc, $D = 4.2$ Mpc), and Hen 2-10 ($r \sim 50$ pc, $D = 12.5$ Mpc) all live in this regime.

Operationally, after 2026-04-24 the selection therefore relies on two layers that $R_\text{pred}$ alone cannot encode:

  1. Geometric hard filter (§5) on $\text{beam}_\text{pc}$, $D$, $i$, $\delta$ — operationally excludes both failure modes by working on $\text{beam}_\text{pc} = \theta_\text{beam} \cdot D$ rather than $D$ alone.
  2. IRAS-colour cut — finds compact sources that $R_\text{pred}$ could not single out from galaxy-integrated SFR.

Anaemic / stripped Gas removed by ram-pressure stripping NGC 4569 (Virgo)

These motivate four hard filters, applied before R_pred / sensitivity ranking:

Criterion Threshold Physics Date added
Inclination $i \geq 47^\circ$ (disk galaxies) $\sec i$ path length insufficient 2026-05-01
Beam physical size $15 \leq \text{beam}_\text{pc} \leq 150$ $<15$ pc: long baseline resolves out; $>150$ pc: noise dilution 2026-05-01
Distance $D \leq 50$ Mpc $1/D^2$ dilution dominates 2026-05-01
Declination $\delta \leq +40^\circ$ Not observable from ALMA 2026-05-13 (post-M82 incident)

Dwarf BCG exception: when HyperLeda MType = Irr / I0 or logR25 is undefined, the inclination criterion is bypassed — the optical axis ratio measures apparent ellipticity, not true disk inclination. NGC 5253 ($i = 68^\circ$) and Hen 2-10 ($i = 40^\circ$) both fall under this carve-out.

7. Workflow notes: skill, download strategy, dual-cube processing

Claude Code and the /alma-query skill

Claude Code is Anthropic's AI programming CLI. A "skill" in Claude Code is a markdown configuration file that defines a reusable, parameterised workflow, invoked at the terminal by typing /alma-query <galaxy>.

The /alma-query skill encapsulates the §2 query workflow as a single command:

  1. NED coordinate resolution + ALMA archive 60″ cone-search.
  2. SPW filtering (require joint coverage of an HRL and a same-band strong-tracer line).
  3. Geometric hard filter (i, beam_pc, D, DEC).
  4. Sensitivity ranking.
  5. Output: ranked report + step-1_download.py download script.

Compared with manual browsing of the ALMA web interface or per-galaxy astroquery scripts, the skill automates and version-controls the entire workflow. Every galaxy is processed with identical selection criteria, and the procedure can be reproduced by any user. Query outputs for all N = 32 sources are archived in the project's git repository.

Download strategy: prefer pre-imaged FITS cubes

The ALMA archive offers two product tiers:

  • Scenario A (raw MS): visibility data, requiring local CASA pipeline calibration and imaging. Each source takes several hours and 10–50 GB of intermediate disk space.
  • Scenario B (pipeline-imaged FITS): archive-completed standard imaging, downloadable directly as *.pbcor.fits and ready for the analysis pipeline.

All N = 32 sources in this work were analysed via Scenario B, in order to validate 30+ sources within the thesis timeline and avoid the time and disk overhead of per-source re-imaging. Scenario A/C candidates (e.g. NGC 4418, an ultra-compact obscured nucleus; NGC 1097, a nuclear-bar starburst) were cancelled or deferred because the CASA pipeline workload was unjustified given low predicted detection probability from other criteria.

NGC 3628 serves as a cautionary case: an early custom v5 imaging washed the signal down to 4.71σ; switching back to the ALMA pipeline standard product gave S/N 15.81 and F = 0.982 Jy·km/s, consistent with Toma's literature value. Do not re-image unless necessary.

When the strong line and weak line are in different cubes

ALMA SPW bandwidth is ~1.875 GHz. Ideally the HRL (e.g. H30α) and the strong-tracer line (e.g. CO(2-1)) fall in the same SPW, so a single cube contains both lines.

In many archival cases, however:

  • The HRL and the tracer fall in two different SPWs of the same ALMA observation → two independent cubes.
  • Or they belong to entirely different ALMA observations → two cubes from different projects.

The dual-cube processing workflow is:

  1. Common-beam convolution: convolve both cubes to a common synthesized beam via radio_beam (the larger beam is the target).
  2. Spatial reprojection: align both cubes to a common pixel grid via reproject.
  3. Mask construction: build the spatial mask on the CO cube using a 3σ threshold, a velocity-integration window, and a morphological-connectivity filter.
  4. Mask transfer: reproject the mask onto the HRL cube.
  5. Flux integration: integrate the HRL pbcor cube within the mask in the velocity direction.

This dual-cube approach allows archival observation pairs in which the HRL and CO are not co-located in a single cube to enter the analysis, significantly expanding the usable archive. NGC 3628 (spw0 = CO(2-1), spw1 = H30α in the same observation) and NGC 5253 X1a (cross-MOUS-ID H30α + CO(2-1) from two separate observations) are both detected through this workflow.
